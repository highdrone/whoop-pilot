Welcome

What Whoop Pilot is

Whoop Pilot is experimental software for supervised FPV research on a Mac. Say “find the cat”, “look in the bedroom” or “go home”, and it plans a route through your house, steers around walls and furniture, and tells you what it saw.

It can
  • Understand plain speech (with Claude) or simple typed commands.
  • Fly missions in your house: go to a room, look in a room, find people, check on pets, patrol, return home.
  • Know where it is by matching its live video against the 3D scan of your house.
  • Notice things that changed since the scan (a new box, a closed door) and fly around them.
  • Remember every flight: where it went, who and what it saw.
  • Let you rehearse every mission first in a simulator of your own house.
It is not
  • Not a security camera. Each flight is a supervised sortie of about 3 minutes (the 480 mAh pack gives about 4).
  • It never arms the drone. A person puts in the battery and arms with the radio.
  • Not hands-off: the Mac, radio and goggles stay on, and the app window must stay visible while it flies.
  • Not for places it hasn’t scanned: it only flies where your 3D scan has seen.

Safety rules
  • The app never arms the drone, and the radio script can’t. You arm with the radio’s own arm switch.
  • If the app goes quiet for 0.3 s, the radio script fails safe: sticks centre and the drone sinks slowly.
  • Keep the app window visible. Browsers slow hidden tabs, so a mission stops if you hide it.
  • The DJI O4 overheats after about 2.5 minutes on the ground with the battery in. Don’t dawdle on the pad.
  • For the first flights, keep people and pets out of the rooms you fly in, and props away from faces.
  • The AI flies in ANGLE (self-level) mode only. The radio forces it while the AI switch is on.

02 · Do this once

One-time setup

About 20 minutes. Nothing to solder, nothing to buy. The full step-by-step is in the app: the Setup guide ↗ link.

1 · The radio (TX15)

  1. Plug the TX15 into the Mac, choose USB Storage, and copy aibrg.lua into SCRIPTS/MIXES on its SD card.
  2. SYS → Hardware → Serial ports → USB-VCP: set it to LUA.
  3. In your Meteor65 model: Model Settings → Enabled Features, turn Mixer Scripts on.
  4. Add aibrg on the Mixer Scripts page. Inputs: the model’s Ail/Ele/Thr/Rud, AI switch SD, push-to-talk SF (optional).
  5. Add the four REPL mix lines for CH1–CH4 and the ANGLE line, as shown in the setup guide.
  6. Bench check, props off: SD up = everything as before. SD down with the app closed = sticks still work, flight mode jumps to ANGLE.
  7. Plug USB-C into the Mac and choose USB Serial (VCP).

2 · The video (Goggles 3)

  1. Install Node.js once: brew install node.
  2. Double-click start.command. Keep its window open: closing it stops Whoop Pilot. Chrome opens the app.
  3. Goggles menu: Settings → About → OTG Wired Connection off.
  4. Share Liveview to Mobile Device via Wi-Fi on. Despite its name, it also turns on the USB live view.
  5. Connect the goggles’ USB-C port to the Mac with a data cable (charge-only cables won’t work).
  6. Set the O4 camera to 4:3.
  7. In the app, choose Real drone. The video appears by itself.

3 · Betaflight / ELRS

  • A switch for ANGLE mode (or use the bridge’s ANGLE line).
  • ELRS telemetry ratio 1:8 or 1:4, then Discover new sensors once on the radio’s Telemetry page.
Optional · Claude

Add an API key in Settings → Brain for real language understanding and picture checks. Without a key, a simple offline parser handles the basic commands and every mission still works. Your key stays in this browser and calls go straight to Anthropic.

04 · Who flies what

Flying modes

Observer

The AI watches and talks, and never moves the drone. Good for checking what it sees.

Co-pilot (default)

You hold the height with the throttle and take off and land when it asks. The AI steers. Recommended starting mode; not a guarantee of safe flight.

Full auto

The AI does everything, including take-off and landing. A whoop has no height sensor, so height comes from the camera. Only after two good Co-pilot flights.

Talking to it

Hold Space (or the mic button, or the radio’s SF button) and speak. Or type in the command bar and press Go. The chips above the bar are one-click examples.

Good to know
  • With a house map, motion is planned, not improvised: Claude picks the mission, the planner flies it.
  • Missions check the battery before every leg and come home with a margin.
  • Without a Claude key, the offline brain understands simple phrases like these. With a key, you can talk normally.
  • After each command, the log shows what Claude cost (usually a fraction of a cent, thanks to caching).

05 · Teach it your home

Your house and its 3D map

Whoop Pilot uses the 3D capture of your house (from SiteSpec or Spacial) as its map and as a 1:1 simulator. It only ever flies through space the scan has actually seen.

  1. Settings → House lists your captures, newest first. Click Use this capture. It copies the capture into the browser, finds the rooms and doors, and builds the flight map.
  2. Click Build the 3D map (about 10 seconds). Your house becomes 5 cm cubes: clear, blocked or never seen. Never-seen space counts as a wall.
  3. Read the coverage table. It says how much of each room the drone can fly in, and which spots the scan missed.
Coverage: 94% of the space the drone can fly in (0.3 to 1.6 m up) is known. Each room gets a plain-language rescan suggestion.
From above. Green: known clear. Pink: never seen. Circles are the biggest gaps; squares show where to stand to rescan.
Rescanning is optional

Gaps just make rooms a little smaller: the drone flies around them. To fill them, walk the scanner slowly around the furniture it names, held low (about 0.7 m: the drone flies lower than you carried the scanner), then import the capture again. Your edits (home pad, names, no-fly zones) are kept.

06 · Mark it up

Home pad, places and no-fly zones

The home pad

Every flight starts and ends here, and it’s how the drone learns where it is. Use the map’s Home tool: click the spot, drag to set the way the nose faces. Print the pad mat from the setup guide and tape it there, arrow matching the map.

Clearance matters. The app wants 0.5 m clear all round and refuses take-off under 0.35 m. Take-offs can drift 30–45 cm, so keep bags and boxes away from the pad.

Places and no-fly zones

  • Landmark: click a spot and name it (“cat bed”), then say “go to the cat bed”.
  • No-fly: click the corners of a zone the drone must never enter.
  • Fly here: click anywhere to send it there.
  • Rename rooms in Settings → House. Ceiling fans and stairs are no-fly automatically.

Let Claude survey the house optional

Claude looks at pictures of your 3D scan, room by room, and suggests room names, places you can send the drone to (“the sofa”) and hazards for a small drone (hanging lamps, plants, cables, glass) as no-fly zones. Nothing changes until you accept it.

Simulator, missions and the HUD

Rehearse in your own house

  1. Simulator → My house. The drone camera is rendered from your scan.
  2. Turn on Rehearse the real flight: it flies exactly as the real drone will, with its camera’s blur, real speed limits and position from video.
  3. Press Reset R, pick a room and press Look in, then Return home.
  4. Turn on People & pets to see how it waits for people. It keeps 1.5 m away from anyone walking.

By hand in the simulator: E arm · Q AI switch · WS throttle · AD yaw · arrow keys pitch and roll.

The mission buttons

Speed limits

The real drone flies at most 0.3 m/s until you measure its braking, 0.4 m/s after, and 0.22 m/s whenever its live depth can’t see. Slow is deliberate: it is a 36 g drone indoors.

09 · It remembers

History, memory and changes

The History tab is the drone’s memory of your house.

Ask it

Type a question or tap a suggestion: “Where did you last see the cat?”, “What changed?”, “Where have you been?”. It answers from what it actually saw, and Claude can use the same memory.

Last seen

People, the cat, the dog and named things, with a picture, the room and when.

Flights

Every flight: time, distance, rooms, findings. Click one to replay its trail on the map and in 3D.

Changes from the 3D scan

Each possible change shows what the drone saw next to what the scan shows, and what Claude thinks if you allowed it to look. Then you decide:

Confirm: it’s thereAdded to the map for good. The drone avoids it.
Dismiss: not realForgotten. The drone flies there again.
A person or petNot a change. It was just someone passing.
Ask Claude againA second opinion, about $0.02.

You can do this mid-flight too: tap the change on the map or the button on the video.

Privacy

History is stored locally. AI commands, approved checks and surveys can send images to Anthropic. Settings → Memory sets how long, and can delete pictures or whole flights.

10 · Before every flight

Ready to fly?

In Real drone mode this checklist opens first. Red items stop missions; orange ones are warnings. Most have a button that fixes them.

Split into what to do before the battery goes in, and what needs it in.

Before you plug in the battery

  • Radio connected over USB-C, its script answering.
  • Home pad set, with room around it.
  • Prepare everything now: the vision models (about 250 MB, once), the 3D map and the position database. A minute or two.

With the battery in

  • ANGLE mode, video from the goggles.
  • Camera calibrated, then the pad check.
  • Braking measured (optional: until then it stays at 0.3 m/s).
  • A clock shows the O4’s time left on the ground.

11 · Three batteries

Your first real flight

Three short packs, in this order. With every new battery: drone on the mat, then press The drone is on its home pad.

A
The day before · at the Mac, no battery

Get everything ready

Import the house and build the 3D map. Set the home pad and tape the mat down. Rehearse Look in and Return home in the simulator. Switch to Real drone and press Prepare everything now. Tidy the floors, lights on, people and pets out. Charge 3 batteries and the goggles.

B
Pack 1 · about 2 minutes · you fly

Record a pad turn and a short hover

  1. Press Record before the battery goes in.
  2. Drone on the mat, nose at the arrow. Battery in, goggles on. Press the pad button.
  3. Turn the drone slowly by hand on the mat, one full circle (about 20 s), then hold still 3 s. This turn calibrates the camera.
  4. AI switch off. Arm, and fly by hand: waist height, a gentle minute, looking at furnished walls.
  5. Land on the mat, disarm, press Record again. Battery out.
The O4 overheats after about 2.5 minutes on the ground. Don’t pause between steps.
C
At the Mac

Calibrate the camera and check the recording

Settings → House → Camera calibration → From a recorded flight → Calibrate from it → Save it. Then Settings → Recordings → Check localization. It passes with at least 3 fixes a second, never lost, and landing within 10 cm of the pad. Don’t go on until it passes. If it fails, the report says why in one line.

11 · Three batteries, continued

Braking, then a real mission

D
Pack 2 · the first time the app flies itself

The pad check, then measure braking

  1. Drone on the mat, battery in, goggles on. Press the pad button: the pad check (about 3 s) matches the camera to the scan.
  2. Choose Full auto, then Measure braking (Settings → Flight). It takes off, flies three short runs at 0.3 m/s in the most open spot, and lands. Thumbs on the sticks.
  3. If it says “There isn’t room here to measure my braking” (it needs a straight 1.6 m), skip it: missions still work at 0.3 m/s.
E
Pack 3 · your first mission

Look in a room, in Co-pilot

  1. Choose Co-pilot, press Record. Drone on the mat, battery in, pad button. No red items in Ready to fly?
  2. Pick a room next to the pad and press Look in. Arm when it asks, flip the AI switch on, take off with the throttle to waist height.
  3. You hold the height, the app steers. It asks you to land at the end.
  4. Afterwards: stop Record, run Check localization on it, and look at History.
F
Later

When Full auto is OK

  • Two Co-pilot missions in a row whose recording check passed, never lost.
  • Braking measured, and take-offs that stayed well clear of everything.
  • The same mission flown cleanly in Rehearse the real flight.

Then try one short Look in in Full auto, over carpet, nobody else in the room, thumbs on the sticks. Watch the height most: it comes from the camera.

After every flight
  • Stop Record and take the battery out (the O4 keeps heating while powered).
  • Glance at History: the path it flew, who it saw, any possible changes to decide.
  • Run Check localization on recorded flights while you’re still learning.
Stop flying if
  • The recording check fails, or the drone says it lost its position more than once.
  • Take-offs drift toward furniture: move the pad somewhere more open.
  • The video stutters or the Mac gets hot and slow: close other apps first.

13 · Claude

Claude, cost and privacy

Claude is the drone’s brain for language and a second pair of eyes. You stay in control of what it sees and what it costs.

AI commands include a camera frame; picture checks and surveys use their consent flow. Review what is sent before using a key.

What gets sent

  • Voice commands: your words, the drone’s state and the current camera frame.
  • Picture checks, only with your OK: a possible change (live vs scan) or an uncertain person or pet.
  • The survey: renders of your 3D scan, with no people in them.

Your choices

Allow once · Always allow · Not now · Never. Change it any time in Settings → Brain: picture checks Ask (default), On or Off.

What it costs

A voice commandusually under a cent (prompt caching)
A change checkabout $0.02
The house surveyabout $0.20 for 3 rooms, shown first
Per-flight cap$0.50 by default (Settings → Brain)

The log shows the cost of every call, split into voice commands, picture checks and memory questions.

Your key

Your API key lives only in this browser. Calls go straight to Anthropic. Claude only advises: the planner and safety layer decide how the drone moves.
