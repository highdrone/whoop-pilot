      First flight, step by step
      1. Import the capture of your house (below).
      2. Build the 3D map and read the coverage: it lists the places the scan missed and how to rescan them. The drone never flies through space the scan didn't see.
      3. Optional: ask Claude to survey the house: room names, landmarks and hazards like ceiling fans, shown with their cost before anything is sent.
      4. Set the home pad with the map's Home tool and tape the printed mat there, its arrow the way the drone faces; keep 0.5 m around it clear.
      5. Rehearse in the simulator: My house → Rehearse the real flight, then Look in a room and Return home.
      6. Prepare before the battery goes in: Real drone → Ready to fly? → Prepare everything now (the models and the position database).
      7. Record a pad turn and a short flight by hand: Record, battery in, “The drone is on its home pad”, one slow full turn by hand on the pad, a minute's hover flown by hand, land on the pad, stop Record. Be quick: the O4 overheats after about 2.5 minutes on the ground.
      8. Calibrate the camera from that recording: Settings → House → Camera calibration → From a recorded flight → Calibrate from it, then Save it.
      9. Check localization on that recording: Settings → Recordings → Check localization; go on only when it passes.
      10. Pad check with the next battery: press “The drone is on its home pad”; the camera must match the 3D scan there.
      11. Measure braking in Full auto, in your largest open room (Settings → Flight); if it says there isn't room, missions stay at 0.3 m/s.
      12. First mission in Co-pilot: “Look in” one room, you hold the height, thumbs on the sticks. Full auto only after two good Co-pilot flights.

      Flights are supervised and about 3 minutes long. The app never arms the drone. Keep this window visible while it flies.

      What to capture, and why it matters

      Whoop Pilot flies your house from a SiteSpec (or Spacial) capture: the 3D scan becomes its map and its simulator, and Claude learns the rooms' names.

      • Walk every room the drone may fly in, slowly, with the doorways between them. Include ceiling fans and stairs: the map keeps the drone away from them.
      • Leave the AprilTags up. Tag 0 on the floor is where the drone takes off and lands (its home pad), and the tags give the scan its true size. Later they let the drone check where it is.
      • Do the tape checks in SiteSpec: measure two or three long distances, wall to wall. The drone keeps about 30 cm from walls, and a scan that is 1% off is already 6 cm off across a 6 m room.
      • Let SiteSpec finish the project (it draws the floor plan with rooms and doors), then use it here. Re-capture after moving big furniture.

        Mac notifications
        Missions say what they find out loud, show it in Events with a picture, and put up a notification so you see it in another app.

        Guarding is supervised: someone puts the battery in and arms the drone (the app never arms it), a sortie lasts about 3 minutes, and this Mac, the radio and the goggles stay on with this window visible.

        The flight memory keeps every flight's path, what the drone saw (with pictures, people included) and the changes it noticed, in this browser only. Claude reads it to answer “where did you last see the cat?”. History (next to Events) shows it.
        New limits apply as soon as you enter them: a shorter one deletes older flights (with their pictures) at once.

        Record flight (at the top) keeps a flight's goggles video, telemetry and commands on this Mac, for checking the lens and the position estimate later. A recording stops by itself after 20 minutes, at 2 GB, when the disk is nearly full, or when this page goes quiet for 2 minutes.
